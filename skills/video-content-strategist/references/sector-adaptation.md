# Sector Adaptation

Every framework in this skill was proven on content about content, but the mechanics (prediction, contrast, trust, matching) are industry-independent. This file is the translation layer: how to turn any business, profession, or language into hooks, angles, proof, and formats.

**When to use:** the user's niche is anything other than social-media marketing; the topic feels boring or regulated; the audience speaks another language.

**When not to use:** the user already has a niche-specific strategy and only needs line edits (use `hooks.md` or `script-structures.md` directly).

---

## 1. The Universal Translation Process

Run these seven steps for any sector before writing a single hook.

| Step | Question | Output |
|------|----------|--------|
| 1. Viewer | Who is the one person who buys? Age, role, situation, budget, what they already believe | A one-paragraph avatar |
| 2. Pain map | Which core pain does the offer solve: money, time, health, or access (to people, places, status, information)? | The core pain |
| 3. One step away | What adjacent, tactical pain leads to the core pain without triggering skepticism? | The pain the videos talk about |
| 4. Topic buckets | Which 3 subtopics does the viewer care about most? (Mine what already performs in the niche) | 3 buckets for the 3-3-1 grid |
| 5. Novelty sources | What changed, what is misunderstood, what is hidden, what is extreme? | An angle bank |
| 6. Proof inventory | What proof exists, by rung: client results, own results, following or co-signs, credentials, third-party | A proof bank (flag gaps) |
| 7. Formats | Which formats can this business shoot easily and credibly? | 3 formats for the grid |

**One step away examples**

| Sector | Core pain | Talk about (one step away) | Avoid leading with |
|--------|-----------|-----------------------------|--------------------|
| Accountant | Money | "Expenses you're allowed to deduct but probably don't" | "Make more money" |
| Personal trainer | Health, status | "Why you're always hungry at 4 p.m." | "Get your dream body" |
| B2B SaaS | Money, time | "The report your sales team rebuilds every Monday" | "Increase revenue 10x" |
| Real estate agent | Money, access | "What your offer letter tells the seller without you knowing" | "Buy your dream home" |
| Lawyer | Money, safety | "The clause most freelancers sign that lets clients pay never" | "Protect your business" |
| Dentist | Health, fear | "The reason your gums bleed even though you brush twice" | "Get a perfect smile" |
| Restaurant | Experience, status | "Why our sauce takes 3 days (and what the shortcut tastes like)" | "Best food in town" |
| E-commerce skincare | Health, appearance | "If moisturizer stings, it's not the brand, it's the order you apply it" | "Look 10 years younger" |

## 2. Novelty Sources for Any Industry

Content works in only two scenarios: the topic is new and you report it, or it is old and you wrap it in a new frame. Every industry has these sources:

| Source | What to look for | Example angle |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Regulation or rule changes | Laws, standards, platform policies changed in the last 6-12 months | "This tax rule changed in January, and most freelancers are still filing the old way." |
| New tools or methods | Equipment, software, techniques | "There's now a way to [procedure] without [the dreaded part]." |
| Price shifts | Something got cheaper, pricier, or newly accessible | "This used to cost $8,000. Now it's under $1,000." |
| Myths | What customers believe that is wrong | "You've been told to [common advice]. It's the reason [problem]." |
| Hidden mechanics | How the work actually happens behind the counter | "Here's what happens to your car after you drop it off." |
| Extremes | The biggest, dirtiest, fastest, most expensive case | "We cleaned the worst kitchen we've seen in 15 years." |
| Seasonal moments | Recurring calendar events in the niche | "Do this 2 weeks before tax season, not during it." |
| Named frameworks | Give your existing method a memorable name (a magic box) | "We call it the 3-Visit Rule." |
| Cult hopping | Tie the niche to a famous brand, person, or movie | "How a top stadium would plan your wedding seating." |

Name your own methods. A named concept turns a generic tip into something new, ownable, and searchable: "the 4-minute dough rule", "the Monday report trap", "the 3-question intake".

## 3. Proof by Sector (and What to Do Without It)

| Sector type | Strongest honest proof | Compliance caution |
|-------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Health and medical | Before/after with consent, procedure counts, years practicing | Avoid guaranteed outcomes; follow local advertising rules for medical claims |
| Finance, tax, legal | Anonymized case outcomes, savings figures with context, credentials | No personalized advice framing; add disclaimers where required |
| Trades and home services | Job footage, time-lapses, customer reactions, reviews | Show real jobs only |
| B2B and SaaS | Dashboards, customer metrics with permission, live demos | Get customer approval for names and numbers |
| Coaches and educators | Student results, your own documented journey | Typical-results disclaimers where required |
| Restaurants and retail | Process footage, sell-out moments, customer queues, reviews | None special beyond honesty |

**Without proof yet:** use the bottom of the ladder honestly (credentials, process transparency, third-party data), build common ground ("I was exactly where you are"), raise production quality, and document your own journey so it becomes owned proof. Never fabricate.

## 4. Format Selection by Business Model

| Business model | High-fit formats | Why |
|----------------|------------------|-----|
| Local service (plumber, cleaner, salon) | Live work execution, extremes, before/after, myth-bust | Visual transformation is built in |
| Professional service (lawyer, accountant, consultant) | Scenario or hypothetical, whiteboard explainer, client-story epiphany, S-tier ranking | Expertise is invisible, so application formats make it visible |
| Healthcare (dentist, clinic, physio) | Procedure explainer, myth-bust, patient question answered, day in the life | Fear and confusion are the main barriers |
| B2B and SaaS | Live breakdown or audit, scenario build, demo experiment, founder epiphany | Buyers need to see the method working |
| Product or e-commerce | Show the factory, experiment or test, comparison ranking, use-case story | Process and proof sell physical goods |
| Food and hospitality | Behind the scenes, process time-lapse, extremes, owner story | Sensory visuals carry the hook |
| Coach or creator | Challenge, hero's journey, live coaching session, framework teach | Transformation is the product |
| Manufacturing or industrial | Show the factory, "how it's made", extreme scale, problem-solution case | Unfamiliar processes create natural curiosity |

## 5. Fill-In Hook Bank (Swap the Brackets)

**Pain and relevance**
- "If you [struggle with pain], stop [common action]. Do [alternative] instead."
- "The reason your [result] isn't working is [named cause]."
- "If you're a [identity] who [situation], this will save you [specific outcome]."

**Contrast and myth**
- "Everyone thinks [belief]. In [N] years of [work], I've seen the opposite."
- "[Common advice] is the fastest way to [bad outcome]."
- "You don't need [assumed requirement] to [outcome]. You need [overlooked thing]."

**Novelty and future**
- "[New thing] just changed how [viewer] [does task]."
- "This is what [process] will look like in [year]."
- "Almost no [professionals] know about this yet."

**Experiment and scenario**
- "I tried [method] on [N] [clients/products] for [time]. Here's what happened."
- "If I had to [goal] from zero with [constraint], here's exactly what I'd do."
- "We [did extreme thing] so you don't have to."

**Size and speed**
- "How we [big result] in [short time] without [usual cost]."
- "[Specific number] [units] in [specific time]. Here's the process."

Always run the result through the 4 S's and the triple alignment tests in `hooks.md`.

## 6. Worked Mini-Briefs Across Sectors

**Physiotherapist (conversion game)**
- Viewer: office workers aged 30-50 with recurring neck pain
- Hook (Contrarian): "If your neck hurts after work, stretching it is making it worse."
- Lock-in (magic box + proof): "It's called the doorway reset. 80% of my desk-job patients start here."
- Format: demonstration (application), filmed in the clinic; text hook: NECK PAIN AFTER WORK
- CTA: comment "neck" for a 3-minute daily routine PDF

**Commercial cleaning company (awareness to local leads)**
- Viewer: restaurant owners in one city
- Hook (Magician + extremes): visual of a grease-caked hood; "This is what's above most restaurant fryers."
- Rehook: "But the scary part isn't the grease. It's what the fire inspector checks next."
- Format: extremes and before/after time-lapse; last dab: "Your fryer is cleaner than your ceiling."

**B2B logistics software (conversion game)**
- Viewer: operations managers at mid-size distributors
- Hook (Investigator): "Most warehouses lose 11% of picking time to one layout mistake."
- Lock-in (tease proof): "We measured it across 40 sites." (placeholder: confirm real data)
- Format: scenario build on a floor-plan graphic, whiteboard style

**Real estate agent (conversion game)**
- Viewer: first-time buyers
- Hook (Teacher): "Here's how to read a listing photo like a home inspector."
- Body: three photo red flags, each with "but" twists; text hook: LISTING RED FLAGS

**Bakery (awareness game, local)**
- Hook (Investigator): "There's a reason this croissant is only sold before 9 a.m."
- Head fake: not scarcity marketing; the lamination stops holding after 4 hours
- Last dab: "So if you're here at 10, you're eating a different croissant."

## 7. Localizing to Other Languages

Frameworks transfer; literal translations of power words usually do not.

1. **Mine local power words.** Run the outlier research in `strategy-and-algorithm.md` on top creators in the target language and niche. Collect the phrases that recur in top hooks.
2. **Keep the grammar, swap the idiom.** Preserve subject, action, objective, contrast; rewrite the amplifier in native phrasing.
3. **Respect formality norms.** Decide between informal and formal address based on the niche (formal address may fit law or medicine; informal fits fitness or food).
4. **Re-check line breaks and length.** Text hooks must still fit two lines; agglutinative languages produce longer words.
5. **Localize references.** Cult hopping needs locally famous brands, people, and places.

**Turkish example (adaptation, not translation)**
- English skeleton: "If you want X, don't do Y, do Z."
- Turkish: "Daha fazla müşteri istiyorsan reklamı artırma. Önce teklifini değiştir."
- English power phrase: "scientifically impossible to skip". A natural Turkish equivalent works better as an idiom: "kaydırması imkânsız", "izlemeden geçemeyeceğin".
- Text hook: "MÜŞTERİ KAYBI (tek cümleyle çözüm)"; keep it two lines, uppercase subject repeater, no punctuation except parentheses or quotes.
- Second person in Turkish is built into the verb ("istiyorsan", "yapıyorsun"); the "you" framing is preserved without the pronoun.

## 8. Sector Adaptation Checklist

- [ ] One avatar written down; core pain identified and approached one step away
- [ ] Three topic buckets validated by what already performs in the niche
- [ ] Angle bank with at least one new reveal, one myth, one hidden mechanic, one extreme
- [ ] At least one named method (magic box) the business can own
- [ ] Proof bank sorted by trust rung, with gaps flagged
- [ ] Formats chosen to fit what the business can film credibly
- [ ] Compliance constraints for the sector respected
- [ ] Language, idioms, references, and formality localized
