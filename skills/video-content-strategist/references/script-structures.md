# Script Structures

Fill-in structures for short-form and long-form video, plus the CTA system and an audit checklist. Hooks are covered in `hooks.md`; loop mechanics in `storytelling.md`.

**When to use:** drafting a full script, reordering points, placing a call to action, or reviewing a script before recording.

**When not to use:** choosing what to make in the first place (load `strategy-and-algorithm.md`).

---

## 1. Short-Form Master Structure (30-90 seconds)

| Block | Length | Job |
|-------|--------|-----|
| Hook | 1-3 lines, 0-5 s | Subject + outcome + contrast (see `hooks.md`) |
| Lock-in zone | 2-3 sentences, 5-15 s | Confirm the claim, anchor trust, intensify curiosity |
| Body block 1 | 10-20 s | First non-obvious insight (first aha) |
| Rehook 1 | 1 sentence | Flip the hourglass, open the next loop |
| Body block 2 | 10-20 s | Second insight (second aha) or the demonstration |
| Rehook 2 | 1 sentence | Only if the video continues |
| Body block 3 (optional) | 10-15 s | Payoff, twist, or application |
| Closing (optional) | 1-2 lines | Takeaway or last dab |
| CTA | 1-2 lines | One aligned lead magnet or next step |

**Fill-in template (education)**

```
HOOK:      [You/your] + [pain or desired outcome] + [contrast].
LOCK-IN:   It's called [named method] / I've used this to [specific result].
BODY 1:    [Insight 1 in one sentence]. [Concrete example].
REHOOK:    But here's the part most people miss...
BODY 2:    [Insight 2]. [Example or on-screen demonstration].
CLOSE:     [One-line takeaway that reframes the topic].
CTA:       Comment "[keyword]" and I'll send you [specific resource tied to this topic].
```

**Fill-in template (news or curiosity, awareness game)**

```
HOOK:      [Subject] just [did surprising thing].
LOCK-IN:   And [double down: it is real, it is bigger than you think].
BODY 1:    The crazy part is... [head fake detail]. [Simple analogy for how it works].
REHOOK:    But the wildest part isn't [obvious thing]. It's [deeper reason].
BODY 2:    [Stakes, why it matters]. There's just one catch... [twist].
CLOSE:     [Memorable last line that could loop back to the hook].
CTA:       Follow for more [category].
```

## 2. Listicle Structure

```
HOOK
LOCK-IN (2-3 sentences)
POINT 1 + POINT 2      (front-load the best material)
REHOOK
POINT 3, 4 (5, 6 optional)
CLOSING (optional)
CTA to aligned lead magnet
```

Rules:
- 3 points minimum, 6 maximum for short-form.
- For each point: name the item, then 1-2 sentences mapping it back to the promised outcome.
- Re-chunk large numbers: "18 tactics" becomes "6 checkpoints with 3 tactics each" so the list does not feel overwhelming.

## 3. Long-Form (YouTube) Writing Order

Write in this order: **packaging, outline, intro, body, outro.**

### 3.1 Packaging (before anything else)
- **Idea:** one line naming the pain point you solve or the rabbit hole you open for one ideal viewer.
- **Title:** must exist before the intro is written; it sets the expectation the intro must confirm. Pattern: dream outcome or pain + explicit modifier ("How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and blow up your content)").
- **Thumbnail:** can stay loose while scripting. Three elements maximum (for example: face, 2-4 words, one graphic). Facial emotion matches the title's tone.
- Best-in-class click-through rates are around 10-12%, so packaging decides most of the outcome.

**Proven long-form title skeletons (adapt to any niche)**
- "How to [outcome] ([named concept])"
- "Give me [N] minutes and I'll [specific result]"
- "I studied [N] [things], these [N] will [outcome]"
- "The ONLY [N] [things] you need to [outcome]"
- "[Topic] isn't hard. It's misunderstood."
- "If I started [goal] from scratch in [year], I'd do this"
- "How I went from [A] to [B] in [time] (nobody teaches this)"
- "[N] mistakes that are killing your [result]"

### 3.2 Outline (gut-check uniqueness)
Bullet the points first. For each, write **What** (what it is), **Why** (why it matters), **How** (how it fits the bigger story). If the points are regurgitated common knowledge, stop and research: the viewer arrives with a mental database of what they already know, and repeating it cannot beat their expectations. Differentiate on at least one axis: **uniqueness** (what you say), **distillation** (how you say it), or **explanation** (the examples and metaphors). Gut check: if this video cost $1,000 to make, would you spend it on this angle?

### 3.3 Intro framework (first 20-60 seconds)
1. **Context:** bluntly state what the video is about (click confirmation).
2. **Common belief:** restate what the viewer currently believes ("This feels like an art that takes years to learn").
3. **Contrarian take:** the contrast ("But there's a formula, and it works every time").
4. **Proof:** why trust you (results, numbers, experience).
5. **Plan:** the ordered roadmap ("the 5-step framework", "6 levels").

Steps 4 and 5 may swap. Optional: pre-empt the obvious objection ("Of course, flashy niches can hold attention. But how does a dentist or a cemetery owner do it?") and promise a live proof later in the video.

### 3.4 Body
- **The first body point must blow their mind.** A weak first point triggers the "scroll the timestamps and leave" behavior.
- **Order by strength: second-best first, best second, then descending.** Two rising points create the pattern that value keeps increasing (music albums rarely put the hit single first).
- **Value loop per point:** *context* (what it is, as simply as possible), *application* (how to do it, with examples), *framing* (why it matters and how it fits the whole).
- **Zoom in, zoom out:** alternate big picture and tactics like a wave.
- **Teaching sequence that works:** theory or analogy first, then a real example, then the tactics.
- **Rehook between points:** "That point was important, but without this next one, the magic is lost."
- **Over-deliver:** "When we get to the end of the list in the title, we're never quite done" (bonus points beat expectations).

### 3.5 Outro
Summarize the points and remind the viewer of the pain you solved ("the free chocolate with the check"). Leave on a high note; likes, shares, and rewatches come from the final impression. Point to one next video or resource.

## 4. The Six Viewer Checkpoints (Whole-Video Psychology)

Every video must pull the viewer through six gates. Checkpoints 1-3 must all happen inside the hook and intro (often within 25 seconds).

| # | Checkpoint | Tactics |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | **Pain acceptance** | Target one of the four core pains (money, time, health, access), but approach it **one step away**: "make more money" trips the skepticism detector, "get more clients from your videos" lets them make the leap themselves. **Twist the knife** (name the frustration bluntly; FOMO or envy of peers). **Audience of one** ("you" far more than "I"). **Pain-solution gap** (show the distance between where they are and where they want to be, with gestures, graphics, or examples) |
| 2 | **Trust** | Proof ranked: results for someone like them > results for yourself > large following or a trusted co-sign > you were in their shoes > common ground > professional presentation. One of the top three is usually enough; otherwise stack the bottom three. Flash visual proof (screenshots, before/after, dashboards) fast. Bigger ask, more trust needed; deeper pain, less trust needed |
| 3 | **Plan of attack** | Point of difference (everyone says Y, you say Z) and structure (a numbered list or steps; structure makes viewers feel smart, and they credit you) |
| 4 | **Likability** | Energy about 50% above normal (record one line at a time so energy never trails off), simplification (make them feel smart), authenticity (a consistent look and genuine enthusiasm) |
| 5 | **Attention** | Create a hunt (tease gold later), rehook with the pain-solution gap, trance rhythm (varied cadence) |
| 6 | **Action** | **Close the loop** (tell them they now have everything to do it themselves; if they want speed, here is the resource; roughly 5% implement alone, 95% pay for speed), **unexpected surprise** (quality beyond expectation), **recap** (tell them what you'll do, do it, tell them what you did) |

## 5. CTA System: Native Embeds

A CTA should feel like part of the content, not an ad break.

1. **Plan it in the outline.** Pick the point where your free resource naturally solves the pain just discussed.
2. **Pattern A, pain then solve:** frame the pain point, then offer the resource as the solution.
3. **Pattern B, dense speedrun:** deliver a framework so fast and deep that a recap resource is genuinely useful ("If you want this as a checklist...").
4. **Bridge phrase:** "If you like how I'm breaking this down, I put my whole [system] into a free [guide]."
5. **Placement:** long-form, about 20-35% in, right after the first major framework lands, plus once at the end. Short-form, at the end only, unless you are actively launching an offer.

**Short-form comment-keyword CTA:** "Comment '[keyword]' and I'll send you [specific resource]." It boosts comments (an algorithm signal) and captures leads by DM. Rules: one keyword, a resource tightly aligned to the video's topic (a checklist, template, swipe file, or AI-ready doc), stated once, at the end. A free, topic-aligned resource draws far more keyword comments than a sales keyword for a paid program (see the data in `creator-teardown.md`); keep sales CTAs for launch windows.

**End-of-video comment prompt:** "Tell me what to cover next; we use the comments to pick our next videos." It turns viewers into collaborators and seeds future ideas.

## 6. Script Formatting and Recording

- One sentence per line; check the jagged right edge for rhythm.
- Mark on-screen text and B-roll in a parallel column.
- Mark every rehook in bold so it survives editing.
- Batch: write a week of scripts in one session, record them in another.
- Record line by line for energy; editors cut between lines.
- Keep a consistent visual setup per series; repetition is recognition, not boredom, because viewers see hundreds of videos between yours.
- Match your delivery demeanor to the audience you want (calm and precise for executives, high energy for beginners).

## 7. Script Audit Checklist

Score each gate pass or fail; rewrite every failing line.

**Hook (0-5 s)**
- [ ] Delay: topic stated in the first sentence
- [ ] Confusion: one interpretation, simple words, active voice
- [ ] Irrelevance: "you" framing, need-to-have pain or desire
- [ ] Disinterest: stated or implied contrast present
- [ ] Triple alignment: passes the sound-off and eyes-closed tests

**Lock-in (5-15 s)**
- [ ] Confirms the hook claim (no bait and switch)
- [ ] Contains proof, a named method, or an objection handle

**Body**
- [ ] First point is the strongest or second strongest
- [ ] Each point has context, application, and framing
- [ ] A rehook every 20-30 seconds; no dead air between sections
- [ ] Beats connect with "but" or "therefore"
- [ ] Storyflow thread never breaks
- [ ] At least two non-obvious insights (double aha)
- [ ] Specific numbers, names, examples instead of generalities
- [ ] Reading level around 6th grade; jargon restated simply

**Close**
- [ ] One CTA, aligned with the topic, native in tone
- [ ] Last line is memorable (and loops, on short-form)

**Integrity**
- [ ] Every claim is true and provable; placeholders flagged
- [ ] No fake urgency or invented testimonials

## 8. Worked Example (B2B, Short-Form, Conversion Game)

**Viewer:** owners of 5-20 person agencies. **Pain:** cash flow (money), approached one step away as "late-paying clients". **Archetype:** Contrarian.

| Section | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual |
|---------|--------|----------------|--------|
| Hook | "If your clients always pay late, your invoices aren't the problem." | LATE PAYMENTS (fixed in 1 line) | Founder at desk, stack of invoices |
| Lock-in | "It's one sentence in your contract. We cut our average payment time from 47 days to 9." | 47 days to 9 days | Before/after chart |
| Body 1 | "Most agencies bill when the work is done. That trains clients to treat you as the last bill." | | Simple timeline graphic |
| Rehook | "But the sentence that changed it isn't about deposits." | | Cut to contract close-up |
| Body 2 | "It's a payment trigger tied to the kickoff call, not the delivery date. Here's the exact wording." | Exact clause on screen | Highlighted clause |
| Close | "You don't need better clients. You need a better trigger." | | Founder to camera |
| CTA | "Comment 'clause' and I'll send you the full contract template." | Comment "clause" | |

Placeholders to confirm with the user: the 47-to-9-day result and the clause text must be real.
