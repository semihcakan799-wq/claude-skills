# Hooks

The hook is the first 3-5 seconds of a video (the first 10-20 seconds on long-form YouTube). Its only job is to make the viewer opt in. Everything in this file is sector-agnostic: swap the subject and outcome for the user's niche.

**When to use:** writing a new opening, fixing a video with a steep drop in the first seconds, writing on-screen text, choosing an opening visual.

**When not to use:** the viewer already stays past 15 seconds but leaves mid-video (load `storytelling.md` and the rehook sections of `script-structures.md` instead).

---

## 1. Interrupt Theory: What a Hook Must Do

A feed scroller is on autopilot ("driving the highway at 70 mph"). The hook triggers an **orienting response**, the involuntary "wait, what was that?" reaction, which creates a **freeze point**: the thumb lifts off the screen.

A hook freezes the viewer when it answers two questions instantly:

| Question | Name | Example answer |
|----------|------|----------------|
| What is this about? | Subject (topic clarity) | "your mortgage", "root canals", "this new AI tool" |
| What will I get if I keep watching? | Outcome (on-target curiosity) | save money, avoid pain, learn the secret, see what happens |

### The 4 S's of hook writing

| Ingredient | Rule | Failure mode |
|------------|------|--------------|
| **Subject** | A crystal-clear person, object, mission, or place | Viewer cannot tell what the video is about |
| **Stakes** | A reason to care. Education: the outcome they want (customers, money, health, time). Entertainment: a scenario they want resolved | Topic is clear but irrelevant |
| **Speed** | Subject and stakes in as few words as possible. Compression, not fast talking. 8 words beat 28 | The brain runs out of room before meaning lands |
| **Super clear** | Only one possible interpretation. No wordplay, no jargon, no vague setups | Half a second of confusion = swipe |

## 2. The 3-Step Hook Formula (Context Lean, Interjection, Snapback)

Works for short-form and long-form, entertainment and education.

1. **Context lean** (1-2 lines) — State the topic bluntly so the right viewer self-selects in, and make them lean in with common ground, a benefit or pain, a metaphor, or a mind-blowing fact.
2. **Scroll-stop interjection** (1 line) — A single stun line built on a contrast word: *but, however, yet, although, but get this, here's the thing*.
3. **Contrarian snapback** (1 line) — Snap the viewer in the opposite direction of the lean, still on topic. The bigger the surprise, the bigger the snap.

Compressed form: **"If you want X, don't do Y, do Z."** Z must genuinely be compelling, or the hook becomes bait.

**Worked examples**

| Sector | Context lean | Interjection | Snapback |
|--------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| Venue tech | "The tech in this stadium is insane. Biggest screen ever built." | "But get this," | "the screen is the least impressive part, because the audio is what blows your mind." |
| Real estate | "There are 3 massive mistakes people make with their mortgage." | "Most people think it's interest rates," | "but it has nothing to do with that. The biggest waste is actually..." |
| Fitness | "If you want to lose belly fat, you've been told to do more cardio." | "Here's the problem." | "Cardio is the slowest lever you have. This one is 3x faster." |
| Bakery | "This is the most ordered pastry in our shop." | "But nobody orders it for the reason you'd think." | "It's the 4-minute rule we use on the dough." |

## 3. The Six Hook Power Words (Grammar of a Hook)

Every strong hook contains four core components and optionally two amplifiers. They can be single words or phrases, and one phrase can serve two roles.

| Component | Type | Typical words | Example: "If I had to grow from 0 to 100K followers again in 90 days, here's how I'd do it" |
|-----------|------|---------------|------|
| Subject | Core | I, we, you, a proper noun | I |
| Action | Core | verb | grow |
| Objective / end state | Core | the outcome, as shocking as possible | 100K followers |
| Contrast | Core | base state vs new state | 0 to 100K |
| Proof | Optional | again, after 10 years, as a surgeon | again |
| Time | Optional | in 90 days, in 30 minutes, right now, today | in 90 days |

Rules:
- Proof and time can move to lines 2-3 if line 1 gets too long.
- Expectation inversion is a contrast too: "If we were smarter, we would listen to 13-year-olds more" (expected: older, wiser people).

## 4. The Six Hook Archetypes

Any idea can be told through any archetype. Write the idea six ways, then keep the one with the most contrast and the best available visual.

| Archetype | Contrast mechanism | Template | Best for |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|----------|
| **Fortune Teller** | Present vs future | "[Thing] is going to change [category] forever." | News, product launches, innovation |
| **Experimenter** | Old way vs tested new way, peer to peer | "I tried [method] for [time]. Here's what happened." | Demos, B2B tools, challenges |
| **Teacher** | You are failing vs here's the method, teacher to student | "Here's how [person/brand] did [result] using [method]." | Authority, expertise selling |
| **Magician** | Visual or verbal stun gun; stackable with the others | "Check this out." / visual pattern interrupt | Any video; combine with another archetype |
| **Investigator** | Unknown secret vs revealed | "Almost nobody knows this about [thing]." | Research-heavy, leading-edge niches |
| **Contrarian** | Conventional wisdom vs your belief, stated directly | "You're doing [thing] wrong. Do this instead." | Experts in crowded categories |

**One idea, six hooks (sector: running shoes)**
- Fortune Teller: "This shoe is going to change how marathoners train."
- Experimenter: "I ran 500 km in this shoe. It has 3 design flaws."
- Teacher: "If you're running your first marathon, this is how to pick a shoe."
- Magician: rapid match cut through 10 shoes, voice over: "Which of these is actually worth it?"
- Investigator: "I can't believe this brand isn't advertising this one feature."
- Contrarian: "Everyone loves this shoe. For daily runs it's completely overhyped."

**Visual pacifier** (a Magician variant): the subject does a passive activity (cooking, stacking cups, driving, applying makeup) that holds just enough subconscious attention to keep the viewer while the spoken hook lands.

## 5. Triple Hook Alignment

Every video has three hooks that must say the same thing:

| Hook | What it is | Importance |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **Text hook** | Designed on-screen words (not the captions) | Highest: eyes hunt for text faster than they parse the image |
| **Spoken hook** | What you say | High |
| **Visual hook** | What is shown | High when strong; talking heads can win on text + spoken alone |
| Caption hook | First line of the post caption, above the fold | Supporting |

**Viewer processing order: visual, then audio, then back to visual** for confirmation (eyes process 10-100x faster than ears). Misalignment causes comprehension loss, the biggest silent killer of hooks. One creator reported two videos on the same channel, with the same strategy, at 14M and 40K views, with alignment as the main difference.

**Alignment tests (run before posting)**
1. **Sound-off test** — Watch with no sound. Can you guess the topic from visual + text alone?
2. **Eyes-closed test** — Listen with eyes closed. Does the audio describe what you would expect to see?
3. **Single-interpretation test** — Read the hook alone. Can it be understood more than one way? Rewrite until no.

### Key-visual-first process
1. List the visuals you have for the first 3-5 seconds. No good visual? Manufacture one (motion graphics, stock, props) or kill the idea.
2. List the most interesting facts and angles; pick the one with the biggest contrast; it decides the archetype.
3. Write the spoken hook (2-4 lines) with the 3-step formula.
4. Write the text hook that supports the visual and its reconfirmation.
5. Watch it back. If visual, speech, visual is not instantly clear, rework before moving on.

For layouts, text-hook placement, caption styling, and pacing, see `visual-techniques.md`.

**Bad vs good (same creator, same channel):** "This is the future of storytelling. It's called a generative world model..." over abstract room visuals flopped (jargon, no concrete visual). "These are called life-size floor plans. You can walk through your exact home design before you build it" over people walking on projected floor plans did 15M views (concrete visual, plain words, text arrow pointing at the visual).

## 6. Text Hook Rules

**Substance: subject repeater + one of three teasers**
- Subject repeater: the name of the core subject or term, ideally capitalized (names are attention magnets)
- Plus one of: a **pain-point reminder** ("PEOPLE PLEASING"), a **dream-outcome teaser** ("make your hooks unskippable"), or a **future-state change** ("Claude just changed social media forever")

**Copy rules**
- Specific beats vague: "22 days" > "a few weeks"; "$2,700" > "a couple thousand"
- Use power words: 1-3 word phrases that amplify ("heat-seeking missiles", "money machine", "scientifically impossible to skip")
- No transition or compound words (and, but, therefore, that)
- No punctuation except quotation marks or parentheses
- Write fragments, not sentences

**Design rules**
- Highly readable font; the platform's system font is fine, or premium typography with a clear size hierarchy
- Place about one third down from the top; never cover eyes or mouth
- Two lines maximum (three only with premium typography where one line dominates)
- Break lines at natural phrase boundaries: "ChatGPT just pioneered / the future of work", never splitting "the future / of work"
- Keep it on screen about 3 seconds so the eye can read, check the visual, and re-read

## 7. Spoken Hook: Formats and Power Words

**Level 1, formats:** the grammatical skeleton of proven hooks (question, tutorial, problem-solution, secret reveal, breakdown, ranking, "here's how to do [big task] in [short time]"). Strip the variables from a winning hook and reuse the skeleton.

**Level 2, power words:** scan your own and competitors' top hooks for amplifying phrases. A phrase that appears in several top performers is double-validated. Then remix it rather than copying:

| Found phrase | Remixes |
|-------------|---------|
| scientifically impossible to skip | psychologically impossible to resist, neurochemically impossible to ignore |
| social media machine | sales machine, referral machine, booking machine |
| the lazy way to | the unfair way to, the cheat-code way to |

Combine a twisted format with twisted power words. The result is new but data-backed.

**Attention triggers to place in the hook or the line after it**
1. Extreme size and speed: a large result in a shockingly short time ("60 days of content in 30 minutes")
2. Specific numbers or dollars ("$342,000", "83 plants per second")
3. Names of people the viewer wants to be like
4. Limited experience, large result ("your first client in your first 30 days")
5. Charts going up and to the right

## 8. The Four Hook Mistakes

A winning hook delivers topic clarity plus on-target curiosity. Failing hooks make one of four mistakes, fixed in this order:

| # | Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---------|---------|-----|
| 1 | **Delay** | Topic first appears on line 3 | Cut the fluff lines; topic in the first 1-2 seconds (speed to value) |
| 2 | **Confusion** | Viewer understands half the words | Fewer, simpler words; active voice; 6th-grade level; one interpretation |
| 3 | **Irrelevance** | Clear topic, but "is this for me?" | Use "you/your", not "I/my"; frame around a need-to-have pain, not a nice-to-have |
| 4 | **Disinterest** | Relevant but not gripping | Add contrast: stated ("Most people use X, but Y is 3x faster") or implied ("This is 8x more effective") |

Examples:
- Delay: "Guys, this is one of the craziest things I've ever seen" becomes "Here are 3 simple ways to fix your gut health."
- Irrelevance: "I've struggled with skin problems my whole life" becomes "If you've struggled with skin problems your whole life..."; "3 trends in skincare" becomes "If you struggle with acne, try these 3 things."

## 9. The Lock-In Zone (2-4 Sentences After the Hook)

The hook is the appetizer; the lock-in zone keeps them for the meal. Psychologically two things happen: the viewer **confirms the hook claim** (no bait and switch) and runs a **rapid trust evaluation** of the messenger.

**Six ways to write it**

| Approach | What you write | Example |
|----------|---------------|---------|
| Tease proof | A stat or result supporting the hook claim | "I used this on 40 clients last year." |
| Tease transformation | The exact before/after or feeling | "You'll go from dreading tax season to finishing in one evening." |
| Clarifying contrast | Contrast against the common belief | "Everybody talks about X. The real lever is these 2 lines right here." |
| Magic box | Name a method they don't know but want | "It's called the 4-minute rule." |
| Objection handling | Bold claim, then the top objection head on | "And no, you don't need a bigger budget." |
| Double down | Intensify the claim and twist the pain | "And if you ignore this, you're overpaying every single month." |

**Four trust levers active during lock-in:** how you look, packaging quality (light, audio, framing, set; care signals trust), relatability of the messenger to the viewer (an accountant viewer trusts the classic accountant look), and **proof**, the trust anchor and strongest lever.

## 10. Long-Form Hooks: Click Confirmation

On YouTube the title and thumbnail set an expectation; the first 10-20 seconds must confirm it and ideally exceed it. Example pattern: title "I spent 100 hours inside a pyramid", intro shows the pyramid immediately (confirm), then adds "with access no one has had before" (exceed). See `script-structures.md` for the full long-form intro framework.

## Quick Hook Checklist

- [ ] Subject named in the first sentence
- [ ] Outcome or stakes clear within 3 seconds
- [ ] Contains contrast (stated or implied)
- [ ] Uses "you" framing and a need-to-have pain or desire
- [ ] Specific numbers, names, or timeframes where possible
- [ ] Spoken, text, and visual hooks pass the sound-off and eyes-closed tests
- [ ] Text hook: max 2 lines, natural line break, no transition words, about 3 seconds on screen
- [ ] Lock-in zone confirms the claim and anchors trust with proof
- [ ] The body can actually deliver what the hook promised
