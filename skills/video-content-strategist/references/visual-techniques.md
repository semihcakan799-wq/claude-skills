# Visual Techniques

What the viewer sees: set, framing, on-screen text, graphics, B-roll, pacing, and thumbnails. Most retention losses are comprehension losses, and the eyes process far faster than the ears, so the visual track must explain the spoken track at every moment. The patterns below were observed frame by frame in Kane Kallaway's top-performing videos and combined with the thumbnail rules he states publicly.

**When to use:** planning B-roll and graphics for a script, briefing an editor or thumbnail designer, choosing a visual identity for a channel, auditing why a video feels slow or confusing.

**When not to use:** the script itself is weak (fix the words first with `hooks.md`, `storytelling.md`, and `script-structures.md`; visuals amplify a script, they cannot rescue one).

---

## 1. Evidence Base

Frames were sampled from seven videos (first 30 seconds at one frame per second, then evenly across the full runtime) and cuts were counted with scene-change detection. A "major visual change" means a new layout, insert, or graphic; same-shot jump cuts are not counted.

| Video | Format | Views | Major visual changes per minute | Changes in first 60 s | Median time between changes |
|-------|--------|-------|-----------------------------|-----------------------|-------------------------------|
| How To Become A Master Storyteller | Long-form, 11 min | 2.9M | 3.1 | 8 | 10 s |
| How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work | Long-form, 17 min | 1.2M | 3.1 | 8 | 13.5 s |
| Give me 15 mins, and I'll make your hooks impossible to skip | Long-form, 16 min | 1.1M | 2.2 | 6 | 15 s |
| How to Create Irresistible Hooks | Long-form, 17 min | 871K | 4.9 | 8 | 8 s |
| The Psychology of Killer Hooks | Long-form, 31 min | 197K | 3.2 | 7 | 10.5 s |
| Team of bots manages my family's health | Short, 110 s | 4.8M (YouTube) | 14.8 | 2 in first 10 s | 2.3 s |
| Plastic bottles turned into cookies | Short, 95 s | 1.95M (Instagram) | 29.6 | 5 in first 10 s | 1.7 s |

Takeaways:
- **Long-form:** a new visual layer roughly every 8-15 seconds (2-5 per minute), denser in the first minute (6-8 changes), with the talking head on screen about half the time.
- **Short-form:** a new visual every 1.5-2.5 seconds; the creator's face is rarely alone on screen.
- Thumbnails were reviewed for about 20 of the top long-form videos.

Limits: seven videos is a small sample; the education Instagram reels were not available as video files, so short-form observations come from the tech account's Shorts (same creator, same studio).

## 2. On-Camera Identity (Constant Across Every Video)

| Element | What he does | Why it works | How to apply in any sector |
|---------|--------------|--------------|----------------------------|
| Set | Dark, moody studio: shelves with props, warm practical lamps, cool neon LED strips as rim and background accents | High contrast and color separate him from the background; the set signals care, which signals trust | Pick one location that says "expert in this field" (clinic, workshop, kitchen, office) and light it with one warm and one cool accent |
| Wardrobe | Same black hoodie and cap in every video | Instant recognition in the feed; "series familiarity" | Choose a signature outfit or color and keep it |
| Framing | Chest-up, near center, eye contact with the lens; podcast microphone visible in frame | Intimate, conversational; the mic signals "expert talk" | Medium close-up; one prop that signals your trade (stethoscope, calculator, chef's knife) |
| Hands | Constant, large gestures: counting on fingers, opening palms, showing size and distance | Gestures add motion (stimulation) and illustrate the pain-to-solution gap | Rehearse gestures for numbers, before/after distance, and size |
| Energy | High, varied delivery recorded line by line | Energy stays high on every sentence | Record one line at a time; editors join the best takes |

## 3. Long-Form Visual System

### 3.1 The first 30 seconds (every top video follows this order)

| Time | Spoken beat | Visual technique |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| 0-1 s | Topic stated | Talking head with an immediate gesture |
| 1-3 s | Topic word | **Kinetic title split around the head**: the topic in huge white capitals, with the head in front of the letters ("STORYTELLING", "SOCIAL MEDIA ALGORITHM") |
| 2-8 s | Context and stakes | **Visualize the sentence**: a simple icon flow ("your content → better stories → better performance") or a wall of example videos with view counters |
| 5-10 s | Common belief shown | **Put the belief on screen**: a mock search result for "25 viral hooks" appears just before he says he won't give a list |
| 8-25 s | Plan / roadmap | **Roadmap as an animated object**: numbered nodes appear along a chrome fishhook (one node per tip), or a numbered list builds beside him |
| 15-30 s | Proof | **Proof montage**: a collage of his past videos, an animated counter ("1,000,000,000+ views"), profile badges with follower counts, or past videos shown as cards with view counts |
| ~25 s | First section | **Chapter card**: "Number 1 / THE DANCE" in glowing type on black, often with a glitch or light-sweep transition |

### 3.2 Per-video motif and palette
Each video gets one **visual mascot** that represents the core concept and recurs throughout, plus an accent palette:
- Algorithm video: a glowing purple orb with nodes ("the algorithm"), on a purple-and-black palette
- Hook videos: a 3D chrome or neon fishhook with numbered nodes; magenta and cyan glow
- Storytelling video: hand-drawn curves and arrows; blue and white

**Transfer:** give every video one object that stands for the idea (a tooth for dental enamel, a padlock for security, a hourglass for time saved) and reuse it for titles, roadmaps, and transitions.

### 3.3 Insert catalog (what fills the other half of the runtime)

| Insert | What it looks like | Use it for |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| Floating card | An external clip in a rounded card with soft glow and a small source caption | Borrowed authority, referenced talks, examples |
| Phone card | A 9:16 vertical video in a phone-shaped card, sometimes beside him | Showing short-form examples |
| Live counter | A tally that increments as he points out each instance ("'But then' moments counter: IIII") | Making the viewer count along; turns a principle into a game |
| Document highlight | A full-screen page of text with key phrases highlighted or underlined in color | Quoting a source, showing a script, showing a prompt |
| Prompt or template box | An on-screen text box with the exact prompt or wording | Saveworthy inputs the viewer will pause for |
| Data visualization | Grids of squares (200 sample viewers, lit = strangers), flow charts (positive/neutral/negative outcomes) | Explaining a mechanism or process |
| Comparison panels | Two columns ("Entertainment content" vs "Education content"), A vs B colored blocks, red squiggle vs green straight arrow | Contrast framing |
| Literal metaphor animation | Pac-Man eating dots for "the brain can only hold 8 words", padlocks for "lock-in", an infinity loop for "curiosity loop" | Making an abstract idea concrete |
| Form matches content | Tips about comments rendered as comment cards with avatars | Instant comprehension of the topic |
| Split layout + building list | Him on one third of the frame, a bullet list on the rest that adds one item per point | Listicles and step-by-step sections |
| Spatial labels | Words floating around his body as he names them ("Set", "Lighting", "Camera angles", "Audio quality") | Enumerations without leaving the talking head |
| Keyword captions | Not full captions: only key phrases appear in the lower third, with the number or key word in bold | Reinforcing the most important words |
| Product UI card | Screen recording in a floating card with glow | Native-embed CTA and tutorials |

### 3.4 Rhythm rules for long-form
- Change the visual layer about every 8-15 seconds; never hold the plain talking head through a dense explanation.
- Density is highest in the first minute, then each section opens with a chapter card or a big kinetic title.
- Every abstract noun gets a picture; every number gets a counter or a chart; every list gets a building list.
- Keep one consistent design system per channel (fonts, glow style, card shape) so inserts feel premium and recognizable.

## 4. Short-Form Visual System

### 4.1 Three layouts in rotation

| Layout | Share of runtime (observed) | Composition | Purpose |
|--------|-----------------------------|-------------|---------|
| **A. Split screen** | ~60% | Top half: B-roll or screen recording that matches the words; bottom half: the creator talking | The viewer sees the subject and the messenger at the same time |
| **B. Full-screen B-roll + face bubble** | ~25% | B-roll fills the frame; the creator's face sits in a small circle in a top corner | Proof moments and visual payoffs ("these are real cookies") |
| **C. Full-face punch-in** | ~15% | Tight close-up of the creator with one huge word on screen | A pattern interrupt every 8-12 seconds that emphasizes a key word |

### 4.2 The text hook (first ~3 seconds only)
- Placed on the seam of the split screen, roughly 40-45% from the top, so it sits where the eye lands.
- Two lines, heavy wide capitals; often the second line in a different color (white then yellow), sometimes on a solid banner box; it slides or pops in.
- Restates the subject plus the outcome ("TURNING PLASTIC / INTO COOKIES"), never a vague teaser.
- Disappears after about 3 seconds, handing over to the captions.

### 4.3 Captions and emphasis
- One to three words per caption, bold, white, small, centered near the seam.
- **Keyword highlight boxes:** important words get a white box with black text ("an expert", "software.").
- **Font swap for emphasis:** on the punch-in shots, a single word becomes huge, and named concepts switch to a serif italic ("Individual Bot Agents", "Cloud Computer") against the sans-serif captions.

### 4.4 B-roll rules
- **Literal matching:** every spoken noun gets its own visual within about 2 seconds ("plastic bottles" → bales of bottles; "NASA" → the logo; "carbon molecules" → a 3D molecule; "Mars" → a Mars landscape).
- **Proof inserts:** news headlines with yellow highlighter strokes, official logos, real product footage, real people using the product.
- **Product shots:** app windows floating at a slight 3D tilt on a colorful gradient.
- **Loop ending:** the last frame shows the same visual as the first frame, so the replay feels seamless.
- **On-screen CTA:** the comment keyword appears as large text ("Comment 'Grok'").

### 4.5 Short-form rhythm rules
- A new visual every 1.5-2.5 seconds.
- Never more than about 10 seconds without a punch-in or layout change.
- The creator's face is always somewhere on screen except during the biggest proof payoffs.

## 5. Thumbnail System

### 5.1 Observed patterns in top long-form thumbnails
- **One to three huge words**, heavy sans-serif, often paired with one serif-italic word ("Addicting *Storytelling*", "KILLER *HOOKS*").
- **Face, chest-up, direct eye contact**, with an expression that matches the promise (a knowing smirk for "irresistible", fingers on temples for "addictive").
- **Object metaphors:** a brain, a fishhook, an eye replacing the "O" in HOOK, his head replacing a letter.
- **Whiteboard diagrams:** grid paper with a hand-drawn curve, blue circles, arrows, and handwritten labels (the 2.9M and 665K thumbnails).
- **Proof grids:** tiles of viral clips with view counts ("▶133M", "12.8M").
- **Contrast splits:** "Normal" with a red tangle vs "System" with a green straight arrow; teal vs red halves.
- **No face can win:** the 1.1M "KILLER HOOKS" thumbnail is a black-and-white brain with a fishhook and no face.

### 5.2 Stated rules (from his thumbnail training)
- **Viewer flow:** visual stun gun (the image grabs), title value hunt (the title explains), visual validation (the eye returns to the image to confirm the promise). Thumbnail, title, thumbnail, in 1-2 seconds.
- **Seven stun elements; use three at most:** color contrast (within the image, or against the niche, such as light thumbnails when competitors are dark), a large face with emotion, a compelling graphic, large text or numbers or dollars, red circles or arrows, aesthetic imagery, a collage.
- **Thumbnail text must not repeat the title.** It names the feeling or outcome ("BASICALLY CHEATING" for a scriptwriting title, not "Scriptwriting Masterclass").
- **Face emotion = the emotion the viewer will feel.** An unknown face can lower trust on a small channel; strong emotion compensates.
- **Graphic options:** the end state (a dashboard or bank balance), the process, before and after, or the pain state.
- **Compositions:** symmetrical, rule-of-thirds, or A/B split.
- **Practical rules:** green means good and red means bad; keep elements out of the bottom-right corner (the timestamp covers it); check legibility at phone size; use shadows to separate face from background; shoot one 4K session of poses and expressions for the designer; keep a swipe file; design about seven options and test the best three.
- **Clickbait is only clickbait when the video fails the promise.** Baiting the click is the job.

## 7. Budget Tiers

| Tier | Long-form | Short-form |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Solo, phone | One lamp plus a colored LED behind you; keyword captions; screenshots with highlighter; chapter cards made in a template | Split screen with stock or phone B-roll; two-line text hook for 3 seconds; 1-3 word captions; a punch-in every 10 seconds |
| Editor | Kinetic titles split around the head, building lists, floating cards, proof montage | Literal B-roll every 2 seconds, keyword highlight boxes, face-bubble layout, loop ending |
| Motion designer | Per-video visual mascot, data visualizations, metaphor animations, consistent design system | 3D product mockups, serif-italic concept titles, custom transitions |

## 8. Visual Audit Checklist

**Short-form**
- [ ] Two-line text hook at the eye line, subject plus outcome, visible about 3 seconds
- [ ] The first visual shows the subject literally (passes the sound-off test)
- [ ] A new visual every 1.5-2.5 seconds; a punch-in at least every 10 seconds
- [ ] Every spoken noun has a matching visual
- [ ] Captions of 1-3 words, with key words highlighted
- [ ] At least one proof insert (headline, data, real footage)
- [ ] CTA keyword shown on screen
- [ ] The last frame loops back to the first

**Long-form**
- [ ] Kinetic topic title within the first 3 seconds
- [ ] Intro sentences visualized (context, belief, roadmap, proof)
- [ ] Proof montage within the first 30 seconds
- [ ] One visual mascot and palette for the video
- [ ] A visual change every 8-15 seconds; chapter cards at each section
- [ ] Lists build on screen; numbers get counters or charts; abstractions get metaphors
- [ ] Inputs worth saving (prompts, templates) shown full screen long enough to pause

**Thumbnail**
- [ ] Three elements or fewer, legible at phone size
- [ ] Text adds the feeling or outcome, never repeats the title
- [ ] Face emotion matches the promise (or a strong object metaphor instead of a face)
- [ ] Contrasts with what competitors' thumbnails look like
- [ ] Several variants designed and tested
